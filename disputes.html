Disputes

When you disagree, there's a clear way through.

Deposit disagreements are common and usually come down to a few items. Only the amount in question stays on hold, and each step has a deadline.

The process

Six steps, each with a deadline

Dispute review is included in the fee. Neither side pays extra to use it.

  1. The landlord proposes a split

    Within 10 days of move-out, the landlord lists each deduction with an amount and a reason.

  2. The tenant challenges what they don't accept

    The tenant has 10 days to accept or challenge each item. They only need to say which items and why.

  3. We pay out everything not in dispute

    Any amount that isn't challenged goes to the right person within 3 working days.

  4. You have 10 days to settle it yourselves

    Many disputes end here. Send offers through your Deposera record, and if you reach agreement, we pay it out.

  5. Each side submits evidence

    If you can't settle, you each get a further 10 days to upload evidence. Both of you can see everything submitted.

  6. An independent reviewer decides

    The reviewer has had no contact with either of you. They send a written decision with reasons within 15 working days of the evidence deadline, and we pay it out within 3 working days.

Evidence

What the reviewer looks at

The deposit belongs to the tenant unless the landlord shows that a deduction is fair, so the landlord has to back up each claim.

Useful evidence

  • The tenancy agreement and any signed changes to it
  • Dated inventories and photos from move-in and move-out
  • Invoices or quotes for cleaning and repairs
  • Messages between you about the problem

Normal wear from everyday living, such as lightly worn carpet, is not a fair reason for a deduction.

Worked example

How the money moves

All figures are an example. The landlord claims £400 for cleaning and carpet damage. The tenant accepts £100 and challenges £300. The reviewer awards the landlord £120 of the £300.
